From 'Paano Kaya Ginawa 'To?' to Building My Own Path

A Personal Journey Towards My IT Career Dreams | January 2026

Minsan naiisip ko, paano kaya ako magiging software developer five years from now? Will I be coding in some big tech company in BGC? Or baka naman I'll be building my own startup with my college batchmates? Honestly, hindi ko pa alam exactly kung saan ako dadalhin ng career path ko, pero alam ko kung saan gusto kong makarating.

Nagsimula lang naman 'tong journey ko sa tech nung napansin ko na palagi akong nag-eenjoy pag nag-customize ako ng phone ko, widgets, launchers, ganun. Tapos isang araw naisip ko, "paano kaya ginawa 'to?" Started watching YouTube tutorials out of curiosity, next thing I know, I'm staying up late trying to make my own simple app. Hindi naman nag-work agad, pero yung feeling na "gets ko na paano 'to gumagana" hooked na ako dun.

Where I Am Now (And Why I'm Both Excited and Terrified)

Currently, I'm in my second year ng BSIT program, and to be honest, parang mixed emotions ako. Excited kasi nag-eenjoy na ako sa major subjects, pero terrified din because nag-start na yung mas malalim na topics. Hindi na 'to basic HTML and CSS lang or simple flowcharts.

Pero looking back sa journey ko so far, nakita ko kung gaano ako nag-evolve. From someone who struggled with basic programming logic, naging someone na who can create working programs na with proper functions and error handling. It's not perfect, and marami pa akong kailangan pag-aralan, but I'm proud of the progress. Yung tipong dati, takot ako sa loops at arrays, ngayon parang okay na ako mag-implement ng mga ganun.

Real talk: May moments talaga na nag-doubt ako sa sarili ko. Like yung time na nahirapan ako sa Data Structures subject, or nung na-realize ko na ang daming hindi ko pa alam compared sa ibang classmates na mas marunong na mag-code. But these moments taught me that growth isn't linear, and that's perfectly okay.

My Five-Year Vision (Ambitious but Grounded)

So ano ba talaga ang gusto kong maging five years from now? If I'm being completely honest, I want to be a full-stack developer working on projects that actually matter to people. Not just coding for the sake of coding, but building solutions that solve real problems.

I imagine myself working sa isang company na values innovation and continuous learning. Sana yung environment is collaborative, where I can learn from senior developers pero at the same time, I can also mentor junior devs or interns. Gusto ko yung culture na hindi ka ma-judge pag nag-tanong ka ng "dumb questions" kasi we all started somewhere, diba?

But here's the exciting part: I also want to have side projects running. Maybe a mobile app that helps students manage their academic life better, or a platform that connects freelancers with local businesses here sa Philippines. Yung tipong passion project na pwedeng maging sustainable business eventually.

"I don't just want to write code. I want to write code that writes stories in people's lives."

The Skills Gap (What's Standing Between Me and My Goals)

Now comes the reality check part. Para marating ko yung vision ko, alam kong I need to level up significantly. Hindi sapat yung basic knowledge na natututunan sa classroom. The tech industry moves fast, and I need to move faster.

Technical Skills na kailangan ko pa i-master:

First, I need to get really good sa web development. Learn HTML, CSS, JavaScript properly, then move to frameworks like React or Vue.js. Currently, okay naman ako sa basics, pero I'm still not confident enough to build a complete website from scratch without relying heavily on tutorials.

Database management is another area na need ko pang i-strengthen. Alam ko ang basics ng SQL, but understanding database optimization, indexing, and handling large-scale data? That's a different ball game. Same goes for cloud technologies like AWS or Azure, sobrang in-demand pero parang ang overwhelming tingnan.

Soft Skills na equally important:

Hindi lang naman tech skills ang kailangan sa industry. I need to work on my communication skills, lalo na sa pag-explain ng technical concepts to non-technical people. Napansin ko sa group projects namin na kahit gaano ka-galing mag-code, if you can't explain your work or collaborate effectively with your team, mahihirapan ka.

Time management and project management skills din. Sobrang iba yung pressure ng real deadlines compared sa academic deadlines. And the ability to receive and give constructive feedback without taking it personally? That's a skill I'm actively developing.

My realistic plan: Instead of trying to learn everything at once (which I tried before and ended up burned out), I'm focusing on mastering the fundamentals first. This semester, strengthen my programming logic and OOP. Next sem, start exploring web development. Small steps, pero consistent.

My Dream Project (The One That Keeps Me Up at Night)

Kung may isang project na gustong-gusto kong gawing reality, it's a comprehensive learning platform specifically designed for Filipino students. Not just another e-learning site, but something that understands our unique educational challenges and cultural context.

Picture this: A platform where students can find resources in both English and Tagalog, where they can connect with tutors na nag-offer ng affordable rates, where they can form study groups with classmates from different schools, and where they can access practice problems tailored to the Philippine curriculum.

Bakit ito? Because I experienced firsthand how hard it is to find quality, accessible, and affordable educational resources. Most platforms are designed for Western education systems. May cultural disconnect. And let's be real, hindi lahat ng students may budget for expensive online courses or tutoring sessions.

This project excites me because it combines my technical skills with my passion for education and helping fellow students. It's ambitious, I know. Baka nga it'll take years before it becomes something substantial. But that's exactly why I'm studying hard now to build the skills I need to make this dream a reality.

The Reality Check (Challenges I'm Preparing For)

Let me be real for a moment. The IT industry in the Philippines is competitive. Sobrang daming talented developers, and many of them have years of experience ahead of me. Imposter syndrome is real, and there are days talaga na pakiramdam ko, hindi ako sapat.

There's also the question of whether I should apply locally or aim for international opportunities. Both have pros and cons. Local companies offer the chance to work on projects that directly impact Filipino users, but international roles often come with better compensation and exposure to cutting-edge technologies.

And then there's the financial pressure. As a student, hindi naman ako mayaman. My family is investing in my education with the hope na makakatulong ako sa kanila eventually. That adds weight to my decisions. I can't afford to just follow my passion blindly without considering practical aspects like salary and job security.

Moving Forward (My Commitment to Myself)

Five years from now, I don't need to be perfect. I don't need to have everything figured out. But I do want to look back at this moment and be proud of the choices I made and the effort I put in.

So here's my commitment: I'll code something every single day, even if it's just for 30 minutes. I'll stop comparing my chapter 1 to someone else's chapter 20. I'll ask for help when I need it because that's not weakness, that's wisdom. I'll take on projects that scare me a little because that's where growth happens.

Most importantly, I'll remember why I started this journey in the first place. It's not just about the salary or the prestige of working sa isang kilalang company. It's about the joy of solving problems, the satisfaction of building something from scratch, and the possibility of making someone's life a little bit easier through technology.

To my fellow IT students: tara, kaya natin 'to. Hindi man tayo perfect, pero at least we're trying. And that's more than enough to start with.

See you sa industry soon. Sana. Haha! 🚀
